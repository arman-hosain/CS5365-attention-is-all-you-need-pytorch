\section{Introduction}

\textbf{Problem and motivation.}
Sequence-to-sequence tasks such as machine translation had long depended on
recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or convolutional architectures. These designs
process tokens sequentially, limiting parallelism and making long-range
dependency modeling difficult.

\textbf{Paper being reproduced.}
\textit{Attention Is All You Need} -- Vaswani, Shazeer, Parmar, Uszkoreit,
Jones, Gomez, Kaiser, and Polosukhin (NeurIPS 2017) -- proposed the
\textbf{Transformer}: the first sequence-to-sequence model built
\emph{entirely} on self-attention, with no recurrence or convolution.

\textbf{Primary method and contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Multi-head self-attention:} 8 parallel attention heads each
    learn different relational patterns across all positions simultaneously.
  \item \textbf{Positional encoding:} sinusoidal functions inject sequence
    order without recurrence, enabling full parallelism.
  \item \textbf{Encoder-decoder architecture:} 6 stacked layers each in the
    encoder and decoder, connected via cross-attention.
  \item \textbf{State-of-the-art results:} 28.4 BLEU on WMT14 EN-DE and
    41.0 BLEU on EN-FR, surpassing all prior models at lower training cost.
\end{itemize}

Our goal was to re-implement the base Transformer in PyTorch, modernize a
legacy codebase for Apple Silicon (PyTorch~2.x MPS), and verify that the
reported training dynamics are reproducible on the smaller Multi30k
DE$\rightarrow$EN dataset ($\sim$29\,k sentence pairs).

% ============================================================
\section{Chosen Result}

We targeted the \textbf{training convergence behavior} described in Section~6
of the paper, visualized in \textbf{Figure~2} (training loss curves), for the
\emph{base model} configuration in \textbf{Table~3}.

The specific result is the \textbf{Noam learning-rate schedule}
(\textbf{Equation~3} of the paper):
\[
  \mathrm{lr} = d_{\mathrm{model}}^{-0.5}\!\cdot\!
    \min\!\left(\mathrm{step}^{-0.5},\;
    \mathrm{step}\cdot\mathrm{warmup}^{-1.5}\right)
\]
which linearly warms up the learning rate for the first \texttt{warmup} steps,
then decays it as the inverse square root of the step number.

\textbf{Why this result?}
The schedule is central to the paper's training claim: that an
attention-only model with correct optimization \emph{converges stably}
and outperforms RNN-based systems. Reproducing this dynamic -- even on a
smaller dataset -- directly validates the core training methodology of the
Transformer and demonstrates that the architecture's behavior is not
dataset-specific.

\section{Methodology}

\textbf{Model architecture.}
We implemented the base Transformer exactly as specified: $d_{\mathrm{model}}
{=}512$, 6 encoder and 6 decoder layers, 8 attention heads,
$d_{\mathrm{ff}}{=}2048$, $d_k{=}d_v{=}64$, dropout~$=0.1$, sinusoidal
positional encodings. Source, target, and pre-softmax projection weights are
shared (Section~3.4 of the paper).

\textbf{Dataset.}
WMT'16 Multi30k DE$\rightarrow$EN ($\sim$29\,k train / $\sim$1\,k validation
pairs), tokenized with spaCy~3.7, shared vocabulary. Multi30k was chosen as
a tractable proxy for WMT14 given hardware constraints; we acknowledge this
limits absolute BLEU comparability.

\textbf{Training setup.}
80 epochs; batch size~256; Adam ($\beta_1{=}0.9$, $\beta_2{=}0.98$,
$\varepsilon{=}10^{-9}$); Noam schedule with \texttt{lr\_mul}$\,{=}\,2.0$,
warmup $= 128{,}000$ steps; label smoothing $\varepsilon{=}0.1$.
Hardware: Apple M4 Pro 24\,GB, PyTorch~2.x MPS backend.

\textbf{Evaluation metrics.}
Token-level \emph{cross-entropy loss}, \emph{perplexity}
($e^{\mathcal{L}}$), and \emph{token accuracy} on both splits per epoch.
BLEU was not computed as beam-search decoding was not activated in this run.

\textbf{Modifications from the original.}
The 2019 codebase (PyTorch~1.3) required four changes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{torchtext API:} \texttt{Field}, \texttt{Dataset}, and
    \texttt{BucketIterator} were removed in torchtext~0.9. We replaced them
    with a custom \texttt{compat.py} shim.
  \item \textbf{PyTorch 1.3$\rightarrow$2.x:} required for MPS (Apple
    Silicon GPU) support.
  \item \textbf{spaCy 2$\rightarrow$3.7:} arm64 wheels unavailable in v2.
  \item \textbf{Removed unused packages:} \texttt{msgpack-python},
    \texttt{msgpack-numpy}, \texttt{terminado} (never imported in source).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What matched the paper.}
The Noam schedule produced the characteristic LR curve from Equation~3:
linear warm-up followed by inverse-square-root decay (Figure~\ref{fig:results}D).
Training perplexity fell monotonically from $\sim$2627 at epoch~0 to 7.28 at
epoch~78, matching the smooth convergence shown in the paper's Figure~2.
Adam with the paper's exact hyperparameters and label smoothing produced
stable, well-behaved loss curves throughout.

\textbf{Discrepancies and challenges.}
Validation perplexity bottomed at 16.02 (epoch~27) then rose, while training
accuracy climbed to 79.4\% -- a clear overfitting signal. This was
expected: Multi30k is $\sim$150$\times$ smaller than WMT14, leaving the
model insufficient data. BLEU could not be directly compared due to the
absence of beam-search decoding. The main engineering challenge was
replacing the deprecated torchtext API; the custom \texttt{compat.py}
shim required careful handling of padding indices, special tokens
(\texttt{<s>}, \texttt{</s>}, \texttt{<blank>}), and dynamic batching to
exactly match the original data pipeline behavior.

\textbf{Broader analysis.}
Despite overfitting on the small corpus, the results confirm the paper's
central claim: multi-head self-attention alone is sufficient to learn
translation structure. The training dynamics are architecture-driven, not
dataset-specific. The full power of the Transformer emerges at scale
(WMT14 / WMT17), where its parallelism makes training feasible in ways
that sequential RNNs cannot match.

\section{Reflections}

\textbf{Lessons learned.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Implementation deepens understanding.} Coding scaled
    dot-product attention clarified \emph{why} $\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling is
    necessary: without it, large dot products saturate the softmax and
    produce near-zero gradients. The Noam schedule similarly made clear
    why warm-up prevents divergence at random initialization.
  \item \textbf{The schedule is non-negotiable.} Early test runs without
    warm-up diverged immediately, confirming the Noam schedule is a core
    ingredient, not an optional improvement.
  \item \textbf{Infrastructure ages fast.} A three-year-old codebase
    required rewriting its entire data pipeline. Long-term reproducibility
    demands pinned environments and thorough dependency documentation --
    not just published model weights.
  \item \textbf{Scale is not a detail.} The gap between Multi30k and WMT14
    explains nearly all discrepancy with the paper. Dropout and label
    smoothing help but cannot substitute for data volume at this model size.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Future directions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Train on WMT14 EN-DE to reproduce the 27.3 BLEU base-model result
    from Table~3 of the paper.
  \item Enable beam-search decoding and compute sacreBleu for a direct
    comparison against the paper's reported scores.
  \item Visualize individual attention heads to study what linguistic
    relationships (e.g., coreference, subject-verb agreement, syntax) each
    of the 8 heads captures.
  \item Implement the \emph{big} Transformer variant to validate the
    scaling results also reported in Table~3.
\end{itemize}
